Published research

EU Regulatory Quality Index: Benchmarking EU legislation against regulatory quality standards

Centre for Liberal Studies – Markos Dragoumis (KEFiM), November 2025 · 84 pages · Contributing researcher

A data-driven benchmark of how well EU directives are written and made. The report pairs a statistical assessment of legal language and length with a composite index, the EU Regulatory Quality Index (EU-RQI), that scores directives from 2022 to 2024 on initiative, subsidiarity, drafting, impact assessment, consultation, and implementation. It closes with evidence-based recommendations for EU lawmakers.

38.6average words per sentence in EU directives, against a 20-word plain-English guideline
66.9average EU-RQI score (0–100), with consultation (49.9) and implementation (47.4) weakest
6.6%of directives were accompanied by a roadmap
44.3%of directives were transposed by the deadline

Why it matters: the report links longer, denser legal texts to lower trust and weaker implementation, and recommends concrete fixes: mandatory early planning, consistent impact-assessment coverage, stronger consultation, and drafting targets for shorter sentences.

My role: as a junior researcher, I worked on directive-level evaluation and Excel-based data tracking.
